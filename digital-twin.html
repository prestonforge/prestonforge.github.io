The model, fully disclosed

t_run = H · (C / (I · H))^k // Peukert runtime (H = 20 h rating period)
V(t) = V_empty + (V_full − V_empty) · SOC(t) − I · R // SOC(t) = max(0, 1 − t / t_run)
t_limp = t_run · (1 − (V_limp − V_empty + I·R) / (V_full − V_empty)) // "never" if the empty flatline sits below the Limp line (I·R < 1 V)

First-order model, deliberately simple and honest about it: linear open-circuit voltage vs SOC, constant internal resistance, constant average current; no temperature, ageing or surface-charge effects. Two modelled failure modes: Limp Mode (terminal voltage touches 21.0 V while charge remains) and going flat (Peukert runtime runs out); when I·R < 1 V the Limp line can never be reached and the binding limit is the Peukert runtime. It's a pacing tool, not a cell datasheet — the interesting engineering is in the trade-off it exposes: every extra amp of average draw costs superlinear minutes (Peukert) and a deeper IR sag that pulls the Limp line closer.
